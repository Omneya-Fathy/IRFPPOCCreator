## Product identity

Willow & Page’s site is a **curated indie bookstore counter moved online**: shoppers discover physical titles by genre, author, and search; staff voice matters through handwritten-style “picks,” not algorithms. It is not Amazon, not a multi-seller marketplace, and not a back-office ERP—it's a trustworthy shop window where **stock honesty** (in-stock or not) and **human curation** are the differentiators. Recognizable without the logo: typographic book spines/covers, shelf-talker staff notes, and calm checkout—not dashboard sidebars or recommendation carousels.

## Explicit RFP constraints

**None stated** for design system, brand colors, typography, or global layout. Functional UI constraints from the RFP: product page fields (cover, title, author, ISBN, price, stock); browse by genre and author; search by title or ISBN; cart and card checkout; staff-curated featured lists (not a recommender); staff price/quantity updates.

## Design opportunity

- Product is fundamentally about: Selling **physical books** from a known indie catalogue with accurate stock and human curation.
- Who is using it: **Book buyers** browsing and purchasing online; **shop staff** updating inventory/pricing and implicitly the picks lists the RFP names.
- Primary emotional goal: **Trust and warmth**—“this is a real shop that won’t sell me a ghost copy,” not anonymous mega-retail.
- Primary functional goal: **Find a title, confirm stock, pay by card**, without Instagram DMs.
- Difference from a generic CRUD application: **Staff picks as editorial voice** and **stock-forward honesty** replace algorithmic feeds and infinite SKU noise.
- Real-world experience this product resembles: **Browsing a neighborhood bookstore**—shelf labels, handwritten staff recommendations, cover-forward displays.
- Visual subjects that naturally belong to this domain: **Book covers (as typographic tiles)**, spine-like vertical rhythm, genre sections, ISBN/metadata, in-stock badges, short curator blurbs.
- Information that deserves visual dominance: **Cover + title + stock state** on browse and product; **staff pick note** where curated lists appear; **price** at decision points.

## Candidate directions

### Direction A — Shelf Talker Counter

- Visual metaphor: Front-of-store table with handwritten recommendation cards clipped to stacks.
- Emotional tone: Neighborly, hand-picked, slightly imperfect (human).
- Visual personality: Warm paper surfaces, dashed “tape” dividers, monospace ISBN in small type, genre chips like section signs.
- Typography personality: Strong serif for titles; humanist sans for UI; italic for curator voice on picks.
- Color strategy: Cream card backgrounds, deep ink text, one brick-red accent for primary actions and in-stock emphasis.
- Composition / layout: Horizontal **staff-picks rail** (talker cards) over a **genre-column catalogue**; product page split: large cover tile left, metadata stack right.
- Imagery / media: `TypographicCover` tiles only; no photographic jackets.
- Interaction model: **Expand shelf-talker** on staff pick rows to reveal curator note (progressive disclosure).
- Signature element: Shelf-talker card that unfolds to show staff quote before “add to cart.”

### Direction B — Literary Journal Catalogue

- Visual metaphor: Quarterly bookseller gazette—editorial spreads and pull quotes.
- Emotional tone: Refined, slow browsing, literary.
- Visual personality: Wide margins, ruled separators, pull-quote typography for picks, minimal chrome.
- Typography personality: Display serif for section heads; light weight body; small caps for author lines.
- Color strategy: Off-white field, charcoal type, single muted teal accent for links/CTAs.
- Composition / layout: **Single-column editorial flow** on home/browse with full-width pick “articles”; catalogue as typographic list rather than dense grid.
- Imagery / media: Large typographic cover as hero on product; picks as mini editorials.
- Interaction model: Scroll-driven **section anchors** (genre as chapter headings); search as inline overlay.
- Signature element: Staff pick rendered as a two-column editorial block with oversized opening quote.

### Direction C — Portland Window Display

- Visual metaphor: After-hours shop window—stacked volumes and featured face-outs.
- Emotional tone: Local, inviting, slightly playful.
- Visual personality: Layered cards with subtle offset shadows, window-frame border on hero pick, badge stickers for “In stock.”
- Typography personality: Rounded sans for UI; condensed caps for genre labels on “shelf edge.”
- Color strategy: Soft gray-green exterior frame, warm interior card white, amber sticker for stock OK.
- Composition / layout: **Asymmetric window collage**—one oversized featured pick, smaller grid of titles; cart as slim slide-over panel metaphor.
- Imagery / media: Typographic covers at varied sizes to simulate face-out vs spine.
- Interaction model: Hover/focus **lift** on window tiles; cart panel slides without leaving browse context.
- Signature element: Featured pick “face-out” tile larger than grid neighbors with sticker stock badge.

## Selected direction

- Chosen: **A — Shelf Talker Counter**
- Novelty (unexpected, product-specific — not random chrome): Translates in-store **handwritten shelf talkers** into expandable UI cards tied to staff picks—a metaphor generic e-commerce rarely uses.
- Relevance (makes sense for this product and users): Mara and Ben explicitly value staff picks and handwritten shelf talkers; buyers need human trust, not algorithms.
- Coherence (one concept, not mixed tropes): Paper/talker/shelf vocabulary runs through picks rail, genre sections, and product metadata—no journal + window mashup.
- Delight (one memorable, useful moment): Opening a talker to read **why** staff recommends a title before adding to cart.
- Usability (readable, discoverable, accessible, responsive): Expand/collapse is keyboard-operable; stock badges are text+color; hierarchy keeps cover/title/stock scannable.
- Feasibility (scaffold Tailwind + `components/ui`; no unapproved kits): Cards, badges, buttons, grids, and `TypographicCover` suffice; no new UI kit.
- Media availability (RFP files / approved upload / typographic or domain-native fallback): No cover art files supplied—**typographic covers** throughout.
- Scope safety (no new screens, roles, fields, or business rules): Uses only catalogue, search, product, cart, checkout, staff picks, and staff price/qty surfaces implied by the RFP.

## Design metaphor

**Independent bookstore counter with shelf talkers**—the web layout feels like standing at the picks table, then stepping into labeled genre aisles.

## Emotional target

Reassured warmth: “Real shop, real stock, real people picked this book.”

## Visual personality

1. Paper-warm surfaces and ink-forward type.  
2. Genre **section signage** (chips/labels), not filter sidebars dressed as enterprise SaaS.  
3. **Stock-forward** badges (in stock / not) at consistent positions.  
4. Curator voice in italic or quoted blocks on picks.  
5. Calm commerce chrome—cart and checkout subdued until needed.

## Composition

**Browse:** Top or upper viewport **staff-picks talker rail** (horizontal scroll on narrow screens), then **genre-led grid** of typographic covers with title, author, price, stock badge. **Product:** Asymmetric two-column—dominant cover tile, stacked metadata and primary add-to-cart. **Staff admin:** Simple table or form rows for price and quantity—structured, flatter density than storefront. Rhythm alternates **wide talker band → denser grid → compact utility** (cart/checkout).

## Visual hierarchy

- **PRIMARY:** Book cover tile + title; staff pick talker headline when on picks rail.  
- **SECONDARY:** Author, price, in-stock state; genre section headers.  
- **SUPPORTING:** ISBN, short curator note body, cart line summaries.  
- **UTILITY:** Nav, search field, secondary links, admin field labels.

## Imagery / media strategy

No RFP image files. All “cover images” are **`TypographicCover`** (fixed ~3:4 aspect, genre accent stripe optional). Staff picks use talker card chrome, not photos. No stock photography, SVG faux jackets, or decorative blobs in `public/`.

## Interaction concept

**Named capability:** staff-curated featured lists. **Interaction:** On staff picks, **expand shelf-talker** to read the staff-written blurb (click/keyboard), then add to cart from the expanded card—mirrors reading a talker before taking the book. Genre browse uses conventional navigation/search without extra novelty.

## Signature moment

A shopper expands a **shelf-talker staff pick** and sees a short human recommendation in curator voice—immediately obviously “indie bookstore,” not generic SaaS.

## Content density

**Moderate** on storefront: populated grids with varied title lengths, multiple genres, 3–5 staff picks with distinct notes, mixed in-stock states. Staff admin **moderate-high** compact rows. Fixtures use realistic Portland-relevant author/title patterns without real customer data.

## Visual rhythm

Large talker cards → medium cover grid → compact cart summary → focused checkout form. Avoid repeating identical card heights in the picks rail; one pick may be visually emphasized per section.

## Controlled asymmetry

Staff-picks rail uses **one slightly larger talker** or offset “tape” edge; product page cover tile larger than metadata column; grid allows one featured row width variation only where staff picks meet catalogue.

## Responsive behavior

Talker rail becomes horizontal scroll with snap; cover grid drops to two columns then one; product stacks cover above metadata but keeps cover as first-viewport anchor; signature expand interaction works with tap and keyboard; admin tables become stacked labeled fields on small screens without dropping price/qty labels.

## Component direction

- **Cards:** Talker cards with top “staff pick” label, dashed or taped edge treatment via border tokens.  
- **Buttons:** Single brick-red primary for add to cart / pay; ghost for expand/collapse talker.  
- **Navigation:** Simple top bar—logo text, search, cart count badge.  
- **Badges:** Pill stock state (in stock / not in stock) with text always visible.  
- **Forms:** Checkout and admin use labeled inputs, clear errors, disabled submit while processing.  
- **Empty/error/success:** Domain copy (“No titles in this genre”) not lorem; success banner after order placed.

## Anti-patterns (this design)

Generic centered hero + three feature cards; purple/blue AI gradients; glassmorphism; fake book cover photos; recommendation “Because you bought…” widgets; marketplace seller rows; loyalty punch-card UI; infinite carousel of algorithms; identical card grid with no talker band; empty whitespace hero with single CTA.

## Acceptance tests

- Memorable moment: User recalls **reading an expandable staff shelf-talker** on a pick—not “red buttons” or “clean layout.”
- Could this be any app if the logo and product name were removed? **No** — because shelf-talker curation, typographic cover tiles, and genre-aisle composition read as **indie bookstore**, not generic CRUD or marketplace templates.
