## Product identity

**Horizon Voyages** is a consultant-led UK leisure travel agency selling **own-brand packages** (not a marketplace). Shoppers are leisure travellers—often mobile, often from paid social—who need to **scan curated packages**, compare **indicative “from” prices**, and either **pay with hosted card fields** or **request a held quote** for a human to close. The experience should feel like a trustworthy high-street agency window digitised: curated rows, clear departure airports (Manchester, Birmingham, London when offered), and honest pricing cues—not an anonymous SaaS dashboard or a flight metasearch clutter board.

## Explicit RFP constraints

- Mobile-first responsive layout.
- Departure airport filter as **dropdown**, not map picker (phase one).
- Product detail must support imagery, descriptions, price/from price, destination, duration, board basis, departure airport.
- Basket, checkout with **hosted payment fields**, and **held-quote** path separate from paid checkout.
- Contact page: UK business hours and bank-holiday treatment (buyer content).
- Merchandising for featured, honeymoon, wedding campaigns.
- Trust strip/footer zones for ATOL and review embeds—**buyer-approved copy only**.
- Imagery: buyer-owned/licensed only; **no faux stock** in POC.
- Brand colours/fonts exist in a deck but are not fully specified in the HTML attachment—retokenize within inferred Premium leisure palette without contradicting future buyer assets.

## Design opportunity

- Product is fundamentally about: **selling agency-built leisure packages** with consultant-grade curation and honest price presentation.
- Who is using it: **public leisure customers** (first-time and repeat), often on phone, comparing against competitors who show packages online.
- Primary emotional goal: **trust and clarity**—“this price and trip are real enough to book or hold.”
- Primary functional goal: **find a package, understand inclusions, add to basket, pay or hold**.
- Difference from a generic CRUD application: **merchandised travel SKUs** with departure airport, board basis, and regulatory trust—not entity admin tables.
- Real-world experience this product resembles: **high-street travel agency window + departure board scan + holiday brochure spine**.
- Visual subjects that naturally belong to this domain: **departure airports, night counts, board badges, “from” prices, ATOL trust, typographic destination tiles** (not fake beaches).
- Information that deserves visual dominance: **destination + duration + departure airport + from-price** on browse; **inclusions and price basis** on detail; **progress and payment/hosted-field zone** on checkout.

## Selected direction

- Chosen: **B — Departure Board Catalog**
- Novelty (unexpected, product-specific — not random chrome): **Pass**
- Relevance (makes sense for this product and users): **Pass** — scan/compare packages and airports matches call-centre selling motion.
- Coherence (one concept, not mixed tropes): **Pass** — board rows, ticket stubs, and filter strip stay one system.
- Delight (one memorable, useful moment): **Pass** — filter-driven “board refresh” makes search tangible.
- Usability (readable, discoverable, accessible, responsive): **Pass** — list rows exceed card grids on small screens; filters collapse to sheet.
- Feasibility (scaffold Tailwind + `components/ui`; no unapproved kits): **Pass**
- Media availability (RFP files / approved upload / typographic or domain-native fallback): **Pass** — typographic covers only.

## Design metaphor

A **holiday departure board** in a Manchester-area travel shop: each line is a leavable package (airport, nights, board, from-price), not a flight number—but the **scan rhythm** is the same.

## Emotional target

Calm confidence: “I can see what’s leaving from my airport, what it costs from, and whether I should pay now or hold for a consultant.”

## Visual personality

- Row-first catalog (not card mosaic).
- Tabular price and nights alignment.
- Ticket-stub trailing price chips.
- Trust strip as a thin **runway bar** above footer (ATOL hook placeholder).
- Campaign accents as **row tags**, not full-bleed heroes.

## Composition

- **Browse/search:** sticky filter strip (dropdown airport prominent per RFP), then stacked board rows with alternating subtle banding.
- **Detail:** single column—typographic cover band, attribute chips, price block with explicit “from” labelling, dual CTAs (add to basket / request hold).
- **Basket/checkout:** narrow progress rail; hosted-field placeholder panel styled as **embossed payment sleeve**; shop picker slot reserved as disabled/select per gap note.
- **Home:** one **featured row** double-height plus campaign tag filters (Featured / Honeymoon / Wedding).

## Visual hierarchy

- **PRIMARY:** Package destination + from-price on browse rows; total/from price on detail and checkout.
- **SECONDARY:** Departure airport, nights, board basis badges.
- **SUPPORTING:** Campaign tags, trust strip, short descriptions.
- **UTILITY:** Nav, cookie/analytics placeholders, legal footer hooks.

## Imagery / media strategy

No RFP image files in attachment. Use **`TypographicCover`** tiles (destination initials, region hue, nights chip) at row-leading 3:2 slots. No SVG beaches, no stock URLs, no `public/` decorative photos.

## Interaction concept

**Capability-backed:** search and filters (dates, budget, board, themes, departure airport dropdown). On filter change, matching rows **re-order with a 200ms highlight sweep** across the row background (respect `prefers-reduced-motion`: instant reorder only). Airport dropdown change updates row leading **airport code pill** emphasis—supports explicit dropdown-not-map requirement.

## Signature moment

The **ticket-stub price tab** on each browse row: a notched trailing element showing **“from £XXX”** and **nights**, visually separating price from destination text like a tear-off stub—memorable and specific to agency package selling, not generic ecommerce.

## Content density

**Moderate:** 6–10 varied package rows on browse, 2–3 basket lines, checkout with realistic UK placeholders (Manchester/Birmingham airports, board basis variety, honeymoon/wedding tags on subset). Avoid identical copy lengths.

## Visual rhythm

Browse: **compact row → compact row → double-height featured row → compact**; detail: **wide cover band → dense chip row → prose → action bar**; checkout: **sparse payment sleeve → dense order summary**. Avoid three equal marketing cards in a row.

## Controlled asymmetry

Featured package row spans full width with larger typographic cover and dual CTAs; standard rows are single-height. Campaign landing uses one **offset typographic cover** left and filter strip right on desktop.

## Responsive behavior

Mobile: filter strip collapses to **“Filters” sheet**; rows become single-column with stub price tab moving **below** destination to preserve thumb reach. Trust strip remains visible above fold on home. Signature stub remains on every row variant.

## Component direction

- **Navigation:** simple top bar with logo text “Horizon Voyages”, links to Browse, Honeymoon, Wedding, Contact.
- **Buttons:** primary solid for “Add to basket” / “Pay securely”; secondary outline for “Request hold quote.”
- **Badges:** board basis (BB/HB/AI), airport codes (MAN/BHX/LHR), campaign tags.
- **Cards:** avoid generic `Card` grids on browse—use **row surfaces** with `border-b` board lines.
- **Forms:** checkout labels left-aligned; hosted fields as bordered iframe placeholders.
- **Empty/error:** “No packages match”—honest copy with telephone CTA placeholder (feeds failure ambiguity acknowledged in fixtures only as static demo state if planned).

## Anti-patterns (this design)

- Centered hero + three equal promo cards.
- Map-based airport picker.
- Purple/blue AI gradient chrome or glassmorphism.
- Marketplace seller avatars or star-rating clutter on every row.
- Fake photographic destinations.
- Identical repeated card grid without row scan line.
- Lorem ipsum or “Test User” strings.

## Acceptance tests

- Memorable moment: User recalls **package rows with tear-off “from” price stubs** and airport pills after one glance at browse—not merely “blue site.”
- Could this be any app if the logo and product name were removed? **No** — because the **departure-board row schema** (airport + nights + board + stub pricing) and **held-quote vs pay** dual path are travel-agency-specific compositions tied to stated capabilities.
